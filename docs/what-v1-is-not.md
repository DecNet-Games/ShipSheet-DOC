# What ShipSheet v1 Is Not
{: .no_toc }

## The boundaries, stated by the product itself
{: .no_toc .text-delta }

---

ShipSheet keeps a provenance record for your content and generates three documents from it. These are the boundaries of v1, printed in the product — in the package's own `Documentation~` folder — so there is no ambiguity about what it does not do. This page reproduces that shipped list.

- **Not a Steam integration.** No Steamworks connection, no submission, no credentials held. ShipSheet produces a draft text file; you submit it.
- **Not legal advice, not a licence verifier.** Records are what you entered. Nothing here verifies that a recorded licence is genuine or certifies a game compliant. ShipSheet reports what your records say. It is not legal advice.
- **Not a code-dependency tool.** UPM packages, DLLs, and plugin licences are out of scope. ShipSheet covers content only: art, models, textures, audio, fonts, video, animations, materials, and text content.
- **Not an AI detector.** ShipSheet records what you declare about AI involvement. It does not scan assets to decide what looks AI-generated.
- **Not a credits screen.** The credits block is a text export and an in-window preview. Your game's credits interface is your own system.
- **No runtime component.** ShipSheet is editor-only. Nothing from this package ships into a player build.
- **No other storefronts' forms in v1.** Steam's Generative-AI disclosure is the only generated regulatory draft. Console and mobile declarations are not templated.
- **No team server, no cloud sync.** The ledger is a project file. Your version control is the sync.

## And in one paragraph, from the listing

ShipSheet generates drafts and keeps records. It never connects to Steamworks, holds no Steam credentials, and submits nothing — you stay the submitter. A generated draft does not guarantee Valve acceptance; Valve judges the game, not the form. The red-flag report flags patterns in your own records; it is record-keeping, not legal advice.
